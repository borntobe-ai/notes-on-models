# Build Your Taste

English | [简体中文](./index_zh-CN.md)

As intelligence becomes faster and more widely available, what we most need to cultivate is taste: the ability to judge what works in AI's output, what falls short, and what is worth keeping. In the past, taste was often honed through apprenticeship, built little by little through foundational work. As AI tools become more common, we have fewer opportunities to do that work ourselves, so we need to cultivate taste more deliberately. I've put together a set of practical steps that I hope can help us build taste across different fields.

## Design the Framework Yourself

A seed has its own pattern of growth as it becomes a tree. A piece of work, too, needs a framework to support its growth. I encourage anyone using AI tools to design the overall framework themselves, rather than always leaving it to AI. Once AI is capable enough, the most important thing to tell it is *why you want to do it this way*. That “why” is your distinctive vision. You don't need to prescribe every detail of the execution. Let AI find its way within your framework.

## Let It Sit

Discussions of the creative process often mention a useful technique: let it sit. When you've just built a product or created something, excitement can make its shortcomings easy to overlook. Even if AI has filled in plenty of detail, that doesn't mean the result is good enough to be considered finished.

Set it aside for a while. Give yourself some time, then come back and examine it. With a little distance, problems you couldn't see before may come into view. You have to notice a problem before you can decide how to fix it. No first draft is perfect. Products and creative work alike need refinement.

## Delete

AI makes generation incredibly cheap. That makes it all the more important to be bold about cutting unnecessary details. A pile of garbage is a pile of garbage, not a work of art. Learning which branches are worth keeping and which to prune is an essential part of developing taste.

## Repeat

Set the framework → Generate → Wait → Prune. Repeat the process, noting the reasons behind each choice and the moments when your judgment changes. Taste takes shape gradually through this cycle.

Finally, the most important thing: haste makes waste. Put your hands in the mud and stir it yourself. That's how you come to feel the substance of the real world.
